1. Summary: outcomes on the pilot presses

The redesign ran on PR-03, PR-06 and PR-09 (the pilot presses) from 2 February 2026, with per-stroke monitoring from 1 April 2026. The other eight presses, unchanged through June, are the comparison. Results compare April to June 2026 with April to June 2025.

In its first quarter the redesign cut scrap and rework on the pilot presses by a third and raised capability on formed characteristics above 1.33, while the comparison presses did not move.

  • Scrap and rework: 2.60% of revenue fell to 1.69%, about $32,000 in the quarter. On the comparison presses it held at 3.32%.
  • Scrapped pieces: the share fell 0.77 points on the pilot presses and 0.19 on the comparison presses.

Production Ppk on critical formed characteristics rose from 1.07 to 1.42 (Section 3.1). Alarm signals fell from 15.3 to 5.3 per 100 subgroups, with an acknowledgement or adjustment recorded on 99% against 65% (Section 4). The per-stroke charts stopped all 6 punch breaks on the stroke of the break (Section 3.3).

Customer PPM and sorting are too few to show an effect in three months. PPM was 267 on 6 complaints, against 191 on 6 in 2025 and 193 on 5 in 2024. There was one sorting event in the quarter of 2025 and none in 2026 (Section 5).

Quality costs booked to the pilot presses fell on scrap, rework and premium freight, and rose on customer charge-backs: $3,734 against $2,925, on the same number of complaints.

3. Results by element of the redesign

3.1 Formed characteristics: three-way charts and the coil-change subgroup

Capability on formed characteristics rose above 1.33 on most characteristics. On the 46 characteristics measured in both quarters, production Ppk rose from 1.07 to 1.42, and those under 1.33 fell from 36 to 10. 39 of the 46 are higher. Cpk rose only from 1.53 to 1.70, and the gap between Cpk and Ppk fell from 0.46 to 0.28, so most of the gain is variation between subgroups removed. Punched characteristics held at 1.57 to 1.63.

Production Ppk by characteristic, April to June 2025 against 2026
Each point is one critical formed characteristic of the pilot presses: its production Ppk from April to June 2025 against April to June 2026, with the equal line and 1.33 on both axes.

The die is centred at a coil change when the coil-change subgroup raises a rule 1 alarm, and rarely otherwise.

  • Beyond three sigma: an adjustment is recorded on 158 of 159 coil-change subgroups.
  • One to three sigma off centre: an adjustment is recorded on 30 of 276. The reaction plan carries no coil-change step.

Coils left off centre stay there. In April to June, 59% of the next five subgroups on a coil left past one sigma fell beyond one sigma on the same side, against 39% after a centring step.

3.2 Sharpening at the count and tool-wear charts

Sharpening at the count has kept burr under its limit on every interval begun under it: all 13 pilot intervals begun after 2 February were sharpened at or before the count, and none crossed the limit. The count applied from each die's next sharpening, so the 8 intervals already running carried on. Five of them passed their count, and two crossed the limit, D-6315-A at twice its count. Of the 21 intervals ending in April to June, the share over the limit is unchanged at 10%, against 9% in 2025. All eight have since been sharpened. Eight other pilot dies are still on intervals begun before 2 February, and D-5884-A is at 1.33 times its count. Burr nonconformances at the press check were 1, against 2.

3.3 Per-stroke monitoring

Per-stroke monitoring cut the parts made between a punch failure and its finding from a median 5,756 under the hourly check to one stroke. The charts stopped the pilot presses 36 times in three months:

  • a worn or chipped punch on 22;
  • slug pulling on 9;
  • nothing found on 5.

Stops with nothing found run at 0.63 per million strokes, against a design ceiling of 2.0.

Punch replacements now follow the charts rather than nonconforming parts. 22 of the 25 replacements in April to June followed a stop and 1 followed nonconforming parts, against 3 of 15 after nonconforming parts in the same months of 2025.

4. Signals, adjustment and the record

Alarms are now fewer and answered: 5.3 per 100 subgroups against 15.3, with an adjustment recorded on 84% against 17%. Every recorded adjustment follows a rule 1 alarm or a coil-change subgroup. The within-coil autocorrelation on adjusted characteristics moved from -0.14 to 0.07.

The record under the charts is restored.

  • Fixtures: the two rebuilt fixtures stand at 7.7% and 9.5% of tolerance.
  • Burr check: it agrees with the gauge on 92% of calls (kappa 0.78).
  • Late entries: 2.1% of gauge-connected entries are made more than 20 minutes after the parts, against 15.4% of keyed entries in 2025.
  • Night shift: planned checks recorded on PR-06 and PR-09 rose from 77% and 72% to 86% and 90%.

Appendix

Method and data

Per-stroke tonnage is charted for autocorrelated data: a first-order autoregressive model is fitted to the run's first 500 production strokes; the EWMA on the per-stroke total is two-sided with lambda 0.02 and limits at 5.5 sigma of the EWMA, its variance from the fitted model; each channel is charted on its share of the total with one residual beyond 5.5 sigma as the signal. The chart parameters were set by run-length computation at autocorrelations of 0.50, 0.60 and 0.70 for a combined in-control average run length of at least 500,000 strokes. Setup strokes, those before the monitor's run start, are set aside. Event counts and complaint counts are compared by the exact Poisson rate test; PPM is pieces rejected over pieces shipped as the customer scored it, with a bootstrap interval over complaints, since rejected pieces arrive in batches; scrapped pieces are compared by the two-proportion score test with the Newcombe interval, shown in the appendix. Capability is as in the assessment report: within-subgroup sigma pooled across subgroups, overall sigma from all readings, medians over characteristics with 60 subgroups or more in the period and a bootstrap interval over characteristics. Gauge R&R is by the ANOVA method with %tolerance at 6 sigma and ndc; attribute agreement with Fleiss' kappa. Outcomes are on the pilot presses for April to June and the comparison presses (the other eight) for the same months, each against the same months of 2025 and 2024. Tonnage in kN; dimensions in millimetres; costs as booked, in US dollars. Data: the SPC software, the press monitor historian (every stroke on the pilot presses), fault events, ERP, QMS, die maintenance log, calibration system, accounting and the gauge study worksheets, as exported to 30 June 2026.
