1. Summary

As of June 30th 2026, there are 1,380 active SPC charts on 11 presses. The charts correspond to the five characteristic classes: formed heights and bend angles, cut geometry (hole position and part length and width), hole diameter, flatness, and sheared edges.

Figure 1: Active SPC Charts by Characteristic Class
1,380 charts on 11 presses, measured and attribute by class; 180 burr characteristics on the sharpening count carry no chart. The one three-way chart on a hole position (C-34479-05) is counted with cut geometry.

The charts as designed raise 133 false alarms a week across the plant, 3.1 per 100 routine subgroups on 4,236 subgroups a week. In the year they ran at 1.30 times that rate on X-bar and R charts, 0.83 on three-way charts and 1.79 on tool-wear charts, each for a reason the record shows.

Two charts changed in the year, both from the record.

  • C-15731-02 (D-5731-A, PR-03): the tool-wear recalculation trigger shortened from 30 to 20 subgroups, first applied 8 December 2025, after the die's intervals shortened under the burr line and the regression lagged them.
  • C-34479-05 (D-4479-C, PR-10): moved from X-bar and R to a three-way chart on 9 March 2026, after 35.2% of its variance was found between coils.

Three findings call for action.

  • 23 of the 45 three-way limit sets issued at the annual review span fewer than ten coil changes. The review computed them on a trailing window rather than the baseline rule. They are re-issued as ten coil changes accrue.
  • Tool-wear charts signal at 1.79 times their expected false-alarm rate on rules 3 and 4, from small level changes at sharpening that pass verification and from the regression line held between verifications. The verification criterion and the re-fit trigger are the recommendations of Section 3.
  • Three-way charts signal at 0.83 of expectation, because their individuals limits carry the residual coil offset left after centring. This is the design working as intended, and the shortfall is the price of it.

2. The charts in force and how they are run

Each class runs on the chart its variation calls for, and every variable chart runs on a gauge in gating status. Formed heights and bend angles run on three-way charts with a coil-change chart beside them; hole diameters on tool-wear charts with limits sloped on shots since sharpening; cut geometry and flatness on X-bar and R; sheared edges and the pin checks on hole diameters on p charts. All 897 variable charts have a current calibration and a gauge study on file, 825 on the gauge itself and 72 through the study of the gauge type at the press. 16 charts are in a baseline and none is past its recalculation trigger.

Figure 3: Charts in force by press and class
Charts in force at 30 June 2026 on each press, by characteristic class, with the press total at the end of each bar.

Limits are set once from a baseline and held across sharpenings under verification, so a chart's limits change only when the process has. A baseline is 25 subgroups; a three-way baseline spans at least ten coil changes. A sharpening does not reset the limits: the first 25 routine subgroups after it verify them, and a failed verification starts a new baseline. Formed charts are verified after a rebuild or a forming-station repair only, since a sharpening does not touch the forming stations. Tool-wear lines are re-fitted at each verification that passes, with the limits' sigma held.

The record holds 1,216 limit sets on 897 variable charts, and 319 new sets were issued in the year: 146 after rebuilds, 74 at the annual review on 19 January 2026, 65 tool-wear lines re-fitted, and 34 after a failed verification. 244 Phase I baselines were completed, 199 X-bar and R and 45 three-way; subgroups beyond the baseline's own limits are screened (94 subgroups in 64 baselines), and Anderson-Darling on the baseline means passes at 5% on 92.9% of them.

Figure 4: New limit sets by month and trigger
New limit sets issued each month, July 2025 to June 2026, by the trigger that issued them; each trigger's year total is in the legend.

The die log carries dates without times, so a run is placed in the interval of the last sharpening dated on or before its start day. 15 of 1,399 runs start on the day their die's sharpening is logged, and 92 span a logged sharpening date, where the log date and the run times disagree; both enter the tool-wear shot counts and the wear rates in Section 4.

The family's 27 charted characteristics run on 20 variable charts and 7 p charts; their register, with each chart's limits basis, gauge, study and status, is in the appendix. F-11333 stands at 16.3% of tolerance on its 5 May 2026 study, with a restudy scheduled for 17 August 2026; F-14026 at 5.3% after its rebuild, and F-15731 at 6.0%.

3. How the charts performed

The rules in force were chosen for the false-alarm load they carry as much as for the shifts they catch, and the year's signals ran close to that design on two chart types and above it on one. Under rule 1 alone an X-bar chart signals once in 370 in-control subgroups; with Western Electric rules 1 to 4 it signals once in 92, and catches a one-sigma shift in 9 subgroups instead of 44. Limits estimated from 25 subgroups move both figures: rule 1 to once in 407, rules 1 to 4 to once in 74.

Takeaway: rules 1 to 4 catch a one-sigma shift five times faster than rule 1 alone, at four times the false alarms.

Verification. 1,308 verifications were completed in the year: 1,100 after sharpenings on X-bar and R charts, of which 34 failed (3.1%); 67 on tool-wear charts, 2 failed (3.0%); and 141 after rebuilds on three-way charts, 3 failed (2.1%). By sharpening, 33 of 408 failed on at least one chart of the die (8.1%). The passes sit close to centre, at a median offset of 0.21 sigma, and 19 of the 1,269 passes lie between 1.0 and 1.5 sigma. On an unchanged process the mean criterion fails by chance on under 0.001% of verifications, so the failures are the dies that came back from the sharpening at a different level.

Takeaway: verification fails on 3% of charts and 8% of sharpenings, and almost every failure is a real level change.

Observed against expected. On charts with no special cause in the interval, the year's signals run at 1.30 times expectation on X-bar and R, 0.83 on three-way and 1.79 on tool-wear, with expectation set at each chart's own baseline size and rules.

  • X-bar and R and tool-wear run above expectation on rules 3 and 4: 1.39 and 2.09 per 100 against 0.45 and 0.78 at known parameters on X-bar and R, 1.20 and 2.37 on tool-wear. Two things in the record produce this. Limits are held across sharpenings, so small persistent offsets (operator reproducibility on the fixture, a part running on another press) accumulate rule 3 and 4 signals that a recomputed chart would absorb. And a level change at sharpening of up to 1.5 sigma passes verification and then runs under the old limits: 14 of 1,131 passes on these charts lie between 1.0 and 1.5 sigma.
  • Three-way runs below expectation because its individuals limits carry the residual coil offset left after centring (Section 4), so rule 1 signals less often than an independent process would.
  • The coil-change chart raised 18 rule 1 signals on 2,292 coils against 12.1 expected on the record's lot structure (1.49 times, with a 95% interval of 0.88 to 2.35). The expectation is above the independent rate because coils arrive in lots of one to four that share 76% of the coil variance.

Takeaway: the excess on X-bar and R and tool-wear charts is on rules 3 and 4, and comes from limits held across sharpenings.

Autocorrelation. Subgroup means within a coil carry a median lag-1 autocorrelation of -0.07 (10th to 90th percentile -0.19 to 0.02). Negative autocorrelation of that size lengthens the in-control ARL under rules 1 to 4 from 92 to 109 and leaves rule 1 unchanged, so the limits need no adjustment for it.

The annual review's three-way sets. 23 of the 45 three-way limit sets issued at the review span one to nine coil changes, against the baseline rule of ten, because the review computed on a trailing window of 50 to 978 subgroups rather than on the baseline rule. Their centre lines are sound; their individuals limits understate the coil offset the design is meant to carry. They are re-issued as ten coil changes accrue on each chart.

Two changes are recommended for the tool-wear charts, where the excess is largest: the verification criterion tightened to 1.0 sigma, since the level change at sharpening is the larger part of it, and the regression re-fitted at each planned trigger as well as at each passed verification.

4. Variance structure and chart design

The chart for each class follows from where its variance lies, and the four measured classes differ enough that no one chart serves them all.

  • Formed height and bend angle: 80.0% of the routine variance lies within the subgroup after centring, 5.2% between subgroups within a coil, and 13.5% is the residual coil offset that centring leaves. Before centring, 49.7% of the incoming variance on coil-change subgroups lies between coils.
  • Hole diameter: 36.2% of the variance is the wear trend over a sharpening interval; 56.1% lies within the subgroup about the trend.
  • Cut geometry: 97.4% within the subgroup, 0.8% between coils.
  • Flatness: 97.5% within the subgroup.
Figure 10: Variance components by class
Each bar is one class: the median share of variance in each component across its critical characteristics (all flatness characteristics) with 60 routine subgroups or more in the year, nested by the method of moments. Between-interval shares are under 0.1% on every class; the wear on hole diameters is the trend share.

The family's characteristics show the same structure one at a time: the formed heights and bend angles carry between-coil shares of 7% to 31% on the routine record and 22% to 52% incoming, the cut geometry characteristics 0% to 6%, and the one variable hole diameter, C-15731-02, a 23.5% wear trend.

Figure 11: Between-coil share of variance by family characteristic
One row per family variable characteristic, labelled by id: its between-coil share of the routine record's variance, coloured by class, against the plant's formed and cut geometry class medians. C-15731-02, the one variable hole diameter, is shown at its wear-trend share.

The coil effect and centring. The coil effect on formed characteristics is predictable from the coil certificate, which is why centring at the coil change removes it. Across 242 formed characteristics, thickness and hardness on the certificate explain the coil means with a median R squared of 0.82; the residual about the fit is 3.2% of tolerance, against coil means that spread 10.1% of tolerance. On C-11333-05 the fit is 2.73 mm of height per mm of certificate thickness and -0.0094 mm per HRB of hardness, R squared 0.814, on 20 coil-change subgroups over 9 coils.

Figure 12: Coil means against the certificate fit, C-11333-05
Each point is one coil-change subgroup (10 pieces, before centring): its mean against the mean predicted from the coil's certificate thickness and hardness; the first subgroup on each coil is the point on the chart of coil-change means. The line is observed equal to predicted.

The three-way design. On formed characteristics the level resets at each coil, and centring on a 10-piece coil-change subgroup leaves each coil with a residual offset of 39.1% of the variance of the subgroup means. Limits from the average moving range do not carry that offset: with them rule 1 alone would signal on 1.57 in-control points per 100, and rules 1 to 4 on 20.5. The individuals limits in force therefore come from the standard deviation of the baseline means, and rules 2 to 4 are off on the individuals chart: rule 1 signals on 0.27 per 100, the nominal rate. The range and moving-range charts run rule 1 at 0.46 and 0.92 per 100. A coil-level shift is the coil-change chart's job, not the routine chart's.

The tool-wear chart. The centre line is the regression of diameter on shots since sharpening over the die's earlier intervals, re-fitted at each verification that passes with the limits' sigma held, so the line follows the die without a new baseline. 5. Per-stroke monitoring design

The per-stroke charts catch a chipped punch on the stroke, where an hourly check would pass up to 11,773 parts on PR-03 before seeing it. The EWMA on the per-stroke tonnage total runs with lambda 0.02 and L 5.5, from a baseline of 500 production strokes at each run start; a residual chart on each channel's share of the total runs beside it at L 5.5.

The design. The total is autocorrelated at lag 1 by a median 0.60 and varies by 1.49% within a run, so the EWMA limits are set on the AR(1)-adjusted variance. At known parameters the in-control ARL is 77.8 million strokes; with the parameters estimated from 500 strokes it is 2.64 million. L is set at 5.5 for the estimated case: at L 4.9 the known-parameter ARL of 3.68 million falls to 312,000 strokes once estimation is allowed for, about one false stop in eight shifts on PR-03.

A chipped punch drops the total by a median 3.06% and moves the largest channel share by 1.32 points. At that shift the EWMA signals in 38 strokes; the channel-share chart signals first on most chips, because the share moves on the stroke of the break. Lambda 0.02 is the choice for shifts of 2% to 3%: at a 2% step it signals in 76 strokes against 117 at lambda 0.05 and 425 at lambda 0.10, with the same in-control ARL.

Figure 17: EWMA ARL1 against shift size, three lambda values
Strokes to signal against a step in the per-stroke total, by the Markov chain method on the AR(1)-adjusted variance at the measured autocorrelation and variation; each lambda's L gives the same in-control ARL; the chipped-punch median shift is marked.

The record. The charts stopped the presses 520 times in the year: 274 on slug pulling, 82 on a worn punch, 71 on a chipped punch and 93 with nothing found. The false-stop rate is 1.02 per million strokes, 82 of the 93 on the EWMA. Strokes were kept for 204 of the stops. On those, chipped punches were stopped at a median 0 strokes from the onset, 90th percentile 20; the EWMA caught 8 chipped punches in the year and the channel-share chart 63, at medians of 38 and 0 strokes on the 4 and 27 with strokes kept.

Parts exposed. Under the per-stroke charts the median chipped punch exposed no parts. Under an hourly check the same failures would have exposed up to 6,499 parts at the median press and up to 11,773 on PR-03, with half that on average; the half-hourly check on the high-speed presses halves both.

A7. Formulas

  • Pooled within-subgroup sigma: the square root of the mean of the subgroup variances. Between/within sigma on three-way charts: the within sigma from the average range (R-bar over d2, d2 = 2.326 for five pieces) combined with the between-subgroup sigma from the average moving range of subgroup means (MR-bar over 1.128, less the within share of a subgroup mean, not below zero).
  • Three-way chart constants: individuals limits at the centre line plus and minus three times the standard deviation of the baseline subgroup means; moving-range limit at D4 = 3.267 times the average moving range; range limit at D4 = 2.114 times the average range; X-bar and R limits at the centre line plus and minus A2 = 0.577 times the average range.
  • ARL by the Markov chain: the states carry the zone history the rules need (the last four points and the run on one side); the ARL is the first element of (I − Q)−11, where Q holds the transitions among states that raise no signal. Limits estimated from a baseline enter as a centre offset, normal with variance 1/m, and a sigma ratio, scaled chi from the average range (3.8 degrees of freedom a subgroup of five), averaged by quadrature. Autocorrelated means carry the previous mean as a discretised state.
  • Variance components: nested by the method of moments on the routine subgroups: within subgroup (pooled), between subgroups within a run and coil, between coils within a sharpening interval, between intervals, each net of the level below it and not below zero.
  • EWMA on AR(1) data: the stationary sigma of the EWMA is the square root of lambda/(2 − lambda) times (1 + a)/(1 − a), with a = phi(1 − lambda); the Brook and Evans chain with 301 states gives the ARL. Estimated parameters: the baseline mean with variance 1/neff, neff = n(1 − phi)/(1 + phi), and a scaled chi sigma ratio; the in-control ARL is the reciprocal of the false-signal rate averaged over the estimates.
  • Coil-change chart limits: from 6 to 19 coils, the mean of the coils on record plus and minus t(0.99865, n − 1) times the square root of 1 + 1/n times their standard deviation; from 20 coils, three standard deviations of the previous 20.
  • The coil-change expectation, Monte Carlo: each charted point is replicated 4,000 times on the lots its coils came from, a lot effect carrying the lot share of the coil variance estimated from the certificate-predicted coil means, plus the coil effect and the measurement of a 10-piece mean; the rate at a point is the share of replicates beyond its limits.
