1. Summary: capability and losses

Production capability on critical formed characteristics is 1.03, against 1.85 filed at PPAP. On 91 of 100 studies the filed Ppk is at least 0.40 above production. Punched characteristics hold closer, at 1.55 against 1.83.

In 2024 scrap and rework were 3.11% of revenue ($1,242,216), customer PPM was 301 on 53 complaints, and 15 sorting events covered 104,804 parts.

The capability gap, the burr nonconformances and the sorting trace to three sources of variation (Section 2). Coil-to-coil shifts carry 55% of the variance on formed characteristics. 54 of the 55 burr nonconformances fell on runs past the die's stated sharpening interval. 12 of the 15 sorting events trace to a die event between two checks.

The charts as run do not detect these sources, and adjustment at the press adds 10% to the variance of the adjusted characteristics (Section 3). Two check fixtures consume more than 30% of tolerance, and 15% of keyed entries are made more than 20 minutes after the parts (Section 4).

2. Sources of variation

2.1 Coil-to-coil shifts

Coil-to-coil shifts are the largest source: 55% of the variance on formed characteristics lies between coils, against 2% on punched characteristics. A coil's level follows the thickness and hardness on its certificate. The fit leaves 2.5% of tolerance about it, against 12.3% between coil means. Charting formed characteristics on limits that carry coil-to-coil variation, with a subgroup and centring step at each coil change, addresses this source.

2.2 Die wear

Burr nonconformances follow late sharpening: 54 of 55 fell on runs past the stated interval, against 17% of all runs. Sharpenings are logged at a median 1.14 times the stated interval, and 48 of 311 intervals carry a burr reading over the limit before the sharpening. On 41 of 124 dies the stated interval is itself longer than the shot count at which burr reaches 80% of its limit. Critical hole diameters grow 0.0043 to 0.0092 mm per 100,000 shots, or 17% to 31% of tolerance over a stated interval.

The burr check at the press does not reliably catch this. Operators agree with the burr gauge on 80% of calls (kappa 0.44), and their reject rates run from 27% to 75% on parts the gauge rejects at 52%. Setting each die's sharpening interval from its burr regression, and qualifying operators against the burr gauge on new limit samples, address these.

2.3 Die events between checks

Sorting follows die events that occur between checks: 12 of the 15 events of 2024. Between two routine checks a press passes a median 1,934 to 16,332 parts at its realised rate, and up to 38,160 at run speed. Where a logged die-protection fault bounds the event, the shop sorted a median 2,948 parts and rejected 77%. Where none does, it sorted back to the previous check, a median 5,541 parts, and rejected 36%. A per-stroke record would bound these events at the stroke.

3. The charts and the response to them

3.1 Limits

The limits in use either absorb the process or signal continuously. On the software presses, rolling limits flag 18.5% of the subgroups that baseline limits flag on formed characteristics and hole diameters. On the paper presses, printed limits signal on 41 of every 100 formed-characteristic subgroups and would signal every 5.3 subgroups with nothing changed. An action is recorded on 28% of paper signals. All 10,688 software alarms were acknowledged, 4,822 with a one-word text and 1,866 with a recorded adjustment.

Fixed within-subgroup limits are not the remedy on formed characteristics. Baseline limits there signal on 43 of 100 subgroups, because the coil shifts of Section 2.1 sit outside them.

3.2 Adjustment

Adjustment at the press adds variation: the adjusted characteristics carry 10.0% more variance over all runs than in their runs with no adjustment (177 of 200 characteristics higher). Their subgroup means carry a within-coil autocorrelation of -0.15, against -0.07 on the others. An estimated 45% of points beyond two sigma are followed by an adjustment, and about half of those are recorded. On the paper presses a point beyond the printed limit keeps 0.81 of its position at the next subgroup, so most are a coil at its own level. A written reaction plan that ties adjustment to a signal or a coil change addresses this.

4. The record under the charts

The readings and their timing are less reliable than the charts assume. The two reworked check fixtures consume 31.2% and 33.6% of tolerance, against 8.9% and 8.4% at PPAP, with about 70% of the gauge variance between operators. Keyed readings end in 0 or 5 at 1.75 times the expected rate. 15.0% of keyed entries are made more than 20 minutes after the parts. The night shift records 84% of planned checks against 94% on days, and the first check of the day shift is recorded on 77% of occasions. Rebuilding the two fixtures and connecting gauges to the terminals addresses the first three.

Appendix

Method and data

Control charts and capability indices follow AIAG SPC, 2nd edition: within-subgroup sigma is the pooled standard deviation across subgroups and overall sigma the standard deviation of all readings; Cpk intervals by the AIAG approximation and Ppk intervals by bootstrap. Stability is by Western Electric rules 1 to 4. Average run length is by the Markov chain method. Variance components are from a nested random-effects model (coil within die, subgroup within coil) fitted by REML. The coil regression is ordinary least squares on the coil's certificate values, with receiving values beside. Gauge R&R follows AIAG MSA, 4th edition, ANOVA method, with %tolerance at 6 sigma and ndc; attribute agreement is with 95% intervals and Fleiss' kappa. Autocorrelation of subgroup means is the lag-1 coefficient of the means less their run-and-coil segment mean, pooled over segments of at least 6 subgroups. Bounded characteristics (burr, flatness) are on the readings as recorded by the percentile method, with the Box-Cox result beside. Proportions carry Wilson intervals. Readings are used as recorded and none is corrected; a keyed reading that cannot be a measurement of the part (further than five tolerance widths from nominal) is set aside, with the count stated. Limits as set are the limits the software computed or the paper form carried at each entry; limits as computed are from a stable 25-subgroup baseline after the most recent sharpening. Press monitor times are aligned to the SPC terminal clock on run start. Data: the SPC software and transcribed paper charts, press monitor fault events, ERP, QMS, die maintenance log, calibration system, accounting and the gauge study worksheets, as exported; record boundaries and coverage are in the appendix.

Appendix 5. Adjustment (Section 3.2)

On keyed entry 23.4% of points beyond two sigma carry a recorded adjustment, and on paper 22.1% of points beyond the printed limit.
