1. Executive summary

The shop's charts and capability hold where variation stays within the subgroup, and fail where it does not. On punched characteristics and flatness, the charts in use signal close to the rate of correctly set limits, and production capability stays above 1.33: Ppk 1.55 on punched characteristics, and Ppu 1.79 on flatness. On formed characteristics, hole diameters and burr, they do not. Production Ppk on critical formed characteristics is 1.04 against 1.84 filed at PPAP. 60 of the 61 burr nonconformances of 2025 fell on runs past the die's stated sharpening interval.

Three sources of variation sit behind these gaps. Coil-to-coil shifts carry 54% of the variance on formed characteristics. Burr and hole size grow with shots since sharpening, and dies are sharpened at a median 1.14 times their stated interval. Die events between routine checks led to 12 of the 14 sorting events of 2025.

Two gaps cut across every class:

  • On the paper presses an action is recorded on 28% of signals, and adjustment at the press adds 10% to the variance of the characteristics it targets.
  • 164 of the 180 check fixtures have no gauge study on file, and the two reworked fixtures that were studied consume more than 30% of tolerance.

In 2025 scrap and rework were 3.12% of revenue ($1,242,201). Customer PPM was 463 on 58 complaints, level with 491 in 2024. 14 sorting events covered 101,154 parts.

2. Scope

The assessment covered every chart in use on all eleven presses in 2025: about 1,550 characteristics on 179 parts, and about 203,000 subgroups. The four high-speed presses chart on paper and the seven medium presses on the SPC software. The assessment drew on seven source systems and ran three studies of its own, from 12 to 15 January 2026: gauge R&R on three check fixtures, and attribute agreement on the burr check. Capability was measured on the 130 critical characteristics with a PPAP study on file, against the production record that followed each study.

3. SPC assessment

Charts, limits and capability hold on punched characteristics and flatness, and do not hold on formed characteristics, hole diameters and burr.

  • Formed characteristics fail on limits, capability and reaction.
  • Hole diameters fail on limits, and their capability is out of line with PPAP.
  • Burr on the lab gauge fails on capability and has no gauge study; the burr check by limit sample is partial on measurement.

Two gaps run across every class: gauge studies cover few of the gauges in use, which leaves measurement short on every class but hole diameters, and signals on the paper presses mostly go unanswered. Plant-wide, recording holds on the day shift and falls short on nights and at the first check of the shift.

3.1 Chart selection and limits

Criterion: each class charted on a chart that fits its variation, with limits from a stable baseline held until a planned trigger.

Chart selection and limits hold on punched characteristics and flatness, and fail on formed characteristics and hole diameters, in opposite ways on the two chart media.

  • Software presses: rolling limits absorb the shifts they should flag. On formed characteristics and hole diameters they flag 17.0% of the subgroups that baseline limits flag.
  • Paper presses: limits printed from the PPAP study signal on 42 of every 100 formed-characteristic subgroups, and an action is recorded on 28% of paper signals.
  • Other punched characteristics: the software runs close to computed limits, at 2.6 signals per 100 subgroups against 3.5. On paper the printed limits signal more often, at 5.4 against 3.6.

Fixed within-subgroup limits are not the remedy on formed characteristics. Computed baseline limits there signal on 42 of 100 subgroups, because the coil shifts of Section 4.1 sit outside them.

Dark columns: the shop's limits as set in 2025; light columns: limits computed from a stable baseline after each sharpening, on the same subgroups.

The printed limits signal whether or not the process has moved. With nothing changed, they signal every 4.5 subgroups on formed characteristics and every 17.0 on hole diameters, against every 31.4 and 38.4 under computed limits.

Printed limits are not recomputed after a sharpening. On 54 of the 57 dies on the paper presses they date from the last rebuild, and every sharpening since has run on them.

3.2 Capability

Criterion: production Ppk of 1.33 or more on critical characteristics, in line with the Ppk filed at PPAP.

Capability holds in production on punched characteristics and flatness. On formed characteristics the filed Ppk overstates production by 0.8: production Ppk is 1.04 against 1.84 filed, and on 86 of 94 studies the filed figure is at least 0.40 above production. Punched characteristics stay capable at 1.55, against 1.85 filed.

Ppk filed at PPAP against production Ppk, by study
Points below the equal line are studies where production capability falls short of the filed figure.

Within punched characteristics, hole position stays closest to PPAP and hole diameter furthest from it.

  • Hole position: hole-to-hole and hole-to-edge run at 1.60 and 1.54, against 1.79 and 1.77 filed, and fall within 0.25 of filed on 22 of 36 studies.
  • Hole diameter: production Ppk is 1.45 against 1.98 filed, within 0.25 on 1 of 16. Cpk on hole diameters is 2.13, so the shortfall is drift between subgroups (Section 4.2), not spread within them.

Flatness is capable, at a Ppu of 1.79. Burr height sits at 1.05.

3.3 Measurement systems

Criterion: every gauge in use studied, with gauge R&R under 10% of tolerance on the check fixtures; attribute checks in agreement with the reference gauge; readings recorded as measured.

Measurement holds where it has been studied, but the studies cover few gauges, and two reworked fixtures and the burr check fall short.

  • Study coverage: before January 2026 the record held a gauge study on 2 of the 180 check fixtures, both from PPAP. The January studies bring that to 16, of which 8 stand under 10% of tolerance.
  • Gauge status: 93% of subgroups were measured on a gauge in service and in calibration. 4.4% were on a gauge away for calibration that day, and 2.7% on one past its calibration due date.

3.4 Sampling frequency and recording

Criterion: checks frequent enough to bound what a die event can pass, recorded on time on every shift.

Check frequency leaves wide windows on the high-speed presses, and recording holds on days but not on nights or at the start of the shift.

  • Window size: between two routine checks a press passes a median 1,934 to 16,333 parts at its realised rate, and up to 38,160 at run speed. On the same 60- and 120-minute frequencies, the high-speed presses pass five to six times what the medium presses pass.
  • What the checks miss: in 2025, 40% of burr nonconformances and 33% of missing or deformed holes were found after the press, at the dock audit or the customer.

The day shift records 94% of planned checks, and nights 84%. The first check of the day shift is recorded on 76% of occasions, against 95% to 97% at every later hour. On nights the medium presses record 80.8%, the lowest of any press group and shift.

3.5 Reaction to signals

Criterion: every signal answered by the reaction its chart prescribes, with adjustments made only on a signal and recorded.

Signals are acknowledged but not acted on consistently, and adjustment at the press adds variation.

  • Software presses: all 10,474 alarms were acknowledged, 4,691 of them with a one-word text.
  • Paper presses: an action is recorded on 28% of signals.
  • Adjusted characteristics: subgroup means swing back and forth within a coil, with a lag-1 autocorrelation of -0.14 against -0.07 on the others.

The adjusted characteristics carry 9.9% more variance over all runs than in their runs with no adjustment, and 165 of 204 are higher.

4. Sources of variation

Three sources of variation explain why the gaps in Section 3 fall on formed characteristics, hole diameters and burr.

4.1 Coil-to-coil shifts

Coil-to-coil shifts are the largest source on formed characteristics. 54% of their variance lies between coils, against 2% on punched characteristics, where 88% lies within the subgroup.

Overall variance by component, formed and punched characteristics
Median shares across studies, nested random-effects model.

A coil's level is predictable from its certificate. Thickness and hardness leave 2.5% of tolerance about the fit, against 12.1% between coil means. Charting formed characteristics on limits that carry coil-to-coil variation, with a centring step at each coil change, addresses this source.

Coil mean against certificate thickness, and the spread about the fit
Coil means of one formed height against certificate thickness (left); spread as a share of tolerance, every study window (right).

4.2 Die wear

Die wear drives the burr nonconformances and the hole-diameter drift. 60 of the 61 burr nonconformances of 2025 fell on runs past the die's stated sharpening interval, against 17% of all runs.

Burr nonconformances by the die's position in its sharpening interval
Shots since sharpening at the end of the run, as a multiple of the stated sharpening interval.

Sharpening runs late, and the stated interval is often set too long. Sharpenings are logged at a median 1.14 times the stated interval. On 41 of 124 dies the stated interval is longer than the shot count at which burr reaches 80% of its limit.

Shot count at 80% of the burr limit against the sharpening interval, by die
Dies below the line are sharpened later than their burr allows.

On die D-1333-C burr reaches 80% of its limit at 364,211 shots, against a stated interval of 400,000 and a median 471,403 as run.

Burr height against shots since sharpening, die D-1333-C
Burr gauge subgroups, 2024 and 2025, as a share of the burr limit.

Hole diameters grow at a steady rate within each interval: 0.0092 mm per 100,000 shots on D-0417-B, and 0.0040 to 0.0094 mm across dies. That is 16% to 32% of tolerance over a stated interval. Setting each die's interval from its burr regression and pulling the die at the count addresses both burr and hole size.

4.3 Die events between checks

Die events between checks cause most sorting, and the check before sets how much is sorted. 12 of the 14 sorting events of 2025 trace to a die event between two checks. Where a die-protection fault bounds the event, the shop sorted a median 3,374 parts. Where none does, it sorted back to the check before, a median 4,996.

Quantity sorted against parts made since the check before
Points on the line sorted everything made since the check before; points below it are bounded by a die-protection fault.

Sorting back to the check before mostly sorts good parts: 37% are rejected, against 77% where a fault bounds the event.

Sorting back to the check before carries the largest share of sorting cost in both years: $3,739 of $8,674 in 2025, and $4,237 of $12,500 in 2024. A per-stroke record would bound these events at the stroke.

Appendix

Method and data

Control charts and capability indices follow AIAG SPC, 2nd edition: within-subgroup sigma is the pooled standard deviation across subgroups and overall sigma the standard deviation of all readings; Cpk intervals by the AIAG approximation and Ppk intervals by bootstrap. Stability is by Western Electric rules 1 to 4. Average run length is by the Markov chain method. Variance components are from a nested random-effects model (coil within die, subgroup within coil) fitted by REML. The coil regression is ordinary least squares on the coil's certificate values, with receiving values beside. Gauge R&R follows AIAG MSA, 4th edition, ANOVA method, with %tolerance at 6 sigma and ndc; attribute agreement is with 95% intervals and Fleiss' kappa. Autocorrelation of subgroup means is the lag-1 coefficient of the means less their run-and-coil segment mean, pooled over segments of at least 6 subgroups. Bounded characteristics (burr, flatness) are on the readings as recorded by the percentile method, with the Box-Cox result beside. Proportions carry Wilson intervals. Readings are used as recorded and none is corrected; a keyed reading that cannot be a measurement of the part (further than five tolerance widths from nominal) is set aside, with the count stated. Limits as set are the limits the software computed or the paper form carried at each entry; limits as computed are from a stable 25-subgroup baseline after the most recent sharpening. Press monitor times are aligned to the SPC terminal clock on run start. Data: the SPC software and transcribed paper charts, press monitor fault events, ERP, QMS, die maintenance log, calibration system, accounting and the gauge study worksheets, as exported; record boundaries and coverage are with each table.
